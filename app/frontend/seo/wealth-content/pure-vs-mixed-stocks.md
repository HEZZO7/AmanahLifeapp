# Pure vs. Mixed Halal Stocks: What's the Real Difference?

Someone invests for two years in stocks that all cleared Sharia screening, and feels entirely settled about it. Then they learn, almost by accident, that they've owed an annual donation on a slice of their returns the whole time — and have never made it once.

That illustrative scenario repeats more often than you'd expect, and the reason is simple: most people read "Sharia-compliant" as the end of the obligation. In reality, most compliant stocks carry an additional annual step, and anyone unaware of it accumulates an unmet obligation without knowing.

*A pure stock keeps all its income compliant; a mixed stock requires purifying a small annual share*

## The Difference in Two Sentences

**Pure play stocks:** companies whose core business is entirely permissible and that generate zero income from non-permissible sources. Nothing further is required after purchase.

**Mixed stocks:** companies with a permissible business that still earn a small share of revenue (under 5%) from non-permissible sources — usually interest on cash reserves held in conventional banks. Investing in them is permissible, but it triggers an annual purification obligation.

Here's the fact that reframes everything: **mixed stocks are the overwhelming majority** of what gets classified as Sharia-compliant today. Genuinely pure stocks are rare, simply because any large company interacts with the conventional financial system somehow — if only through an operating bank account.

Put plainly: if you hold compliant stocks, statistically they're probably mixed, and purification probably applies to you.

## Why "Permissible" Isn't the Whole Answer

The permissibility of holding a mixed stock doesn't make its non-compliant income slice permissible by extension. A broad consensus of scholars and standards bodies — AAOIFI among them — treats purification as obligatory, not optional.

And here's a detail people get wrong: donating the purification amount **earns no reward**. It's removing an impurity from wealth, not charity in the ordinary sense. Conflating the two leaves someone believing they've given generously when they've actually just met an obligation.

## Checking for Yourself — Five Real Minutes

The easy path: tools like Zoya or Musaffa display each stock's classification (pure/mixed) and the exact purification percentage owed, calculated from the latest audited filings. No manual math at all.

But understanding the logic behind the number is worth two minutes:

Picture a car company that also runs a financing arm lending to customers at interest. If 98% of revenue comes from car sales and 2% from financing interest, then **2% of any dividend you receive** needs purifying. That figure is the "purification ratio," typically published by Sharia index providers.

For larger portfolios holding several mixed stocks, the smarter approach is tracking each stock's ratio separately (it shifts quarterly) and consolidating into one annual record alongside your zakat calculation — rather than computing each dividend as it lands.

## Dividends or Capital Gains? Here Scholars Genuinely Differ

Stock returns split into two categories, treated differently:

**Dividends:** purified at the rate of the company's non-compliant revenue share for that period. Little disagreement here.

**Capital gains:** when you sell at a profit, **scholarly opinion is genuinely split**. Some Sharia boards require purifying part of that gain; others limit the obligation to dividends alone.

This isn't a simplification of a marginal dispute — it's a real division among recognized authorities. And if your portfolio leans on capital appreciation rather than dividend income (as many growth-oriented portfolios do), this point deserves an individual consultation rather than personal judgment.

## Three Common Mistakes

**Assuming "compliant" means "done."** Most compliant stocks are mixed, not pure, and purification is an integral part of the obligation.

**Skipping purification because the amount is small.** The obligation is principle-based, not proportional. Ten dollars gets purified the same way ten thousand does.

**Confusing purification with zakat.** Purification removes an impurity from specific income; zakat is an annual obligation on underlying wealth once it meets nisab and a lunar year passes. Two entirely separate obligations, even if calculating both in one annual sitting saves time.

## FAQ

**Can I just avoid mixed stocks and hold only pure ones?**
Theoretically yes, practically very difficult. The universe of genuinely pure stocks is narrow, and restricting yourself to it shrinks diversification in a way that hurts your risk profile more than it helps.

**Who calculates the purification ratio for me?**
Established screening tools (Zoya, Musaffa) publish it per stock automatically, updated with each new financial report.

**Is purification obligatory or optional?**
The majority of scholars and standards bodies, AAOIFI included, treat it as obligatory when holding a mixed stock.

**I forgot to purify previous years — what now?**
Calculate the accumulated amount and donate it once you notice. If the period is long or the sums significant, consulting a qualified scholar beats estimating on your own.
