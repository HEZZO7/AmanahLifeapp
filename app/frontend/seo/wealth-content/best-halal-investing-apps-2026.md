# Best Halal Investing Apps and Platforms in 2026: A Complete Comparison

If you asked "what's the best halal investing app?" expecting a single name, the honest answer will disappoint you: **there's no single best app for everyone.**

The reason is simple: "best" depends entirely on what kind of investor you are. Someone who wants to screen stocks themselves needs a completely different tool from someone who wants a portfolio managed for them with zero manual involvement. This guide sorts tools by actual need, not a random ranked list.

![Visual classification of halal investing tools: screening tools, managed platforms, and alternative markets](/assets/wealth/diagram-app-categories-en.svg)
*Three genuinely different categories, each serving a different type of investor*

## Category One: Screening Tools — For Building Your Own Portfolio

These tools don't manage your money; they tell you whether a given stock is Sharia-compliant, leaving you to execute the purchase through any regular broker.

### Zoya
The cleanest interface and easiest to use of the three. Syncs directly with U.S. brokers to track your portfolio automatically, and includes a purification calculator and an annual zakat tool. Paid subscription is nominal (around $1/month), while free searches are limited.
**Best for:** anyone wanting speed and clarity without complexity, investing primarily in the U.S. market.

### Musaffa
Much broader coverage (200,000+ securities globally) and deeper educational content. Feels more like a full research platform than a simple consumer app — suited to investors who want to understand the "why," not just get a quick verdict.
**Best for:** the serious investor who wants to dig into the underlying numbers.

### Islamicly
The strongest coverage of non-U.S. markets — UK, India, Southeast Asia. Uses the DJIM methodology (close to AAOIFI but not identical). Subscription-only, with no meaningful free tier.
**Best for:** anyone investing across multiple international markets outside the U.S.

## Category Two: Fully Managed Platforms — For a Ready-Made Portfolio

Here you don't pick stocks yourself; you answer simple questions (risk tolerance, time horizon) and the platform builds and manages the entire portfolio.

### Wahed Invest
The world's first fully digital halal investing platform, available in over 150 countries. Automatically invests in a mix of halal equities, sukuk, and gold, under a qualified Sharia board. Relatively low minimum to start (around $100), reasonable annual fee (around 0.49%).
**Best for:** beginners who want zero manual involvement — "invest and forget."

### Sarwa
A UAE-based robo-advisor offering an Islamic portfolio alongside its conventional one, under an independent Sharia board. Strongest presence specifically in the Gulf region.
**Best for:** investors in the Gulf wanting a locally-rooted experience with regional customer support.

## Category Three: Alternative Markets — For Those Past the Basics

### Cur8 (from the Islamic Finance Guru team)
Opens access to opportunities traditionally reserved for institutional investors: startups, real estate, and fixed-income sukuk. Only suitable for those who understand alternative asset dynamics and can commit to longer holding periods.
**Best for:** advanced investors who've already built a solid core (stocks/funds) and want additional diversification beyond the public market.

## How to Actually Choose Between Them

Ask yourself one question: **do you have the time and inclination to review your own holdings every quarter?**

If yes, start with Zoya or Musaffa, and execute purchases through any regular broker supporting fractional shares (as covered in our guide to starting with $100). If no, Wahed is the more logical starting point — it removes the review burden entirely.

**A hybrid model many advanced investors actually use:** Zoya or Musaffa for quick screening, keeping a portion of the portfolio in Wahed as a managed core, and adding Cur8 later as an extra diversification layer once the core portfolio has matured.

## One Thing Worth Not Ignoring

All these tools' fees and geographic availability change constantly. Check each tool's official site before subscribing — don't treat any article's numbers, including this one, as a permanent fixed fact.

## FAQ

**Do I actually need more than one tool?**
Not necessarily at the start. One screening tool (Zoya or Musaffa) is entirely sufficient for a beginner. Combining several tools only becomes worthwhile as a portfolio grows and becomes more complex.

**Do these tools replace an individual Sharia consultation?**
Not in every case — see our guide on when you need a Sharia advisor to understand the difference between standard cases and situations needing individual judgment.

**Do managed platforms like Wahed calculate zakat automatically?**
Some offer estimates or helper tools, but independent verification remains your responsibility — see our full guide to calculating zakat on stocks to confirm the right method for your situation.

**Which is actually cheaper long-term?**
Screening tools alone are usually cheaper (small fixed subscription fees), but require your time to execute trades yourself. Managed platforms charge a percentage of your total portfolio — which can become a larger absolute amount as your portfolio grows, despite the convenience.
